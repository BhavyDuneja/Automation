# Dedicated expert vs freelancer vs agency: which should you hire?

You need a specialist: a growth marketer, an AI engineer, a case manager. You can hire a freelancer, brief an agency, or add a dedicated expert to your team. All three work. Each one fails when it is used for the wrong job.

This guide compares the three on what matters: how long the work lasts, who manages it, what you are really paying for and what happens if it is not working. It also says when a dedicated expert, the model we run at AnantaSutra, is the wrong choice.

## Dedicated expert, freelancer or agency: which is better?

It depends on the work. A freelancer suits a short, well-defined task; an agency suits a complex project that needs project management; a dedicated expert suits ongoing work where continuity matters. ## When a freelancer is the right call

A freelancer fits when:

- the task has a clear end, such as a logo, a one-off audit or a landing page;
- you can write the brief and judge the result yourself;
- it does not matter if the person is busy elsewhere next quarter.

The trade-off: availability and continuity depend on the freelancer, and what they learned about your business leaves when the task ends.

## When an agency is the right call

An agency fits when:

- you want a result and do not want to manage anyone day to day, such as a campaign or a website build;
- the work needs several skills at once, such as strategy, design and development;
- the scope is fixed, so you can judge success on delivery.

The trade-off: you often see one price rather than what is inside it, and the people doing the work can change. Ask for the breakdown anyway.

## When a dedicated expert is the right call

A dedicated expert fits when:

- the work will run for months, not weeks, such as ongoing growth marketing, an AI engineer who builds and maintains your workflows, or a case manager for a steady flow of cases;
- the person needs to learn your business to be effective;
- you want them in your team's Slack, WhatsApp and standups, working on your priorities;
- you can give them enough direction.

The trade-off: you give more direction than you would to an agency, and a dedicated expert is the wrong tool for a one-week task. If that is your situation, hire a freelancer.

## What does each option cost?

Cost differs more in logic than in level. A freelancer is paid for a task or for hours. An agency is paid for an outcome, and its price covers the people, process and overhead behind it. A dedicated expert is paid as a person, month after month.

## Five questions to ask before you choose

Whichever option you consider, ask:

1. What is the person's actual cost, and what is the provider's own fee, shown separately?
2. Is there a minimum term, and can I pause?
3. If it is not working, how fast is the replacement, and is that in writing?
4. Who employs the person, and who handles payroll, PF and TDS?
5. Has the provider placed someone in a business like mine?

A freelancer is one person hired for a defined task, usually paid per task or per hour. An agency is a team that sells you an outcome on a retainer or fixed scope.
