# Email 2 of 5: agency vs freelancer vs dedicated expert (Day 3)

Subject option A is the default (question). B is the contrarian angle and C is benefit-driven.

## Body (copy from here)

Hi {{first_name}},

There are three common ways to add a specialist to your team. Each is right in some situations and wrong in others.

A freelancer. One person, usually paid per task or per hour, and a good fit for a clearly defined, short job. The trade-off: freelancers often have other clients, so availability and continuity depend on them.

An agency. A team that sells you an outcome, usually on a retainer or a fixed scope, and a good fit when you want a result without managing anyone. The trade-off: you often see one price rather than what is inside it, and the people doing the work can change.

A dedicated expert. One specialist who works inside your team, on your priorities, week after week, which suits ongoing work where context matters. The trade-off: you give more direction than you would to an agency, and it is the wrong tool for a one-week task.

My rule of thumb: if the work will be done in a few weeks, hire a freelancer or an agency. If it will run for months and the person needs to learn your business, a dedicated expert is usually the better fit.

That second case is the model we run, across 12+ domains from engineering and AI to legal and healthcare. You interview the experts we shortlist, the one you choose works as part of your team, and one person at AnantaSutra stays accountable.

If you are not sure which of the three you need, bring your situation to the call. I would rather tell you that a freelancer or an agency fits better than place the wrong person.
